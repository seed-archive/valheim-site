# Deep North

**What do I need from the Deep North?** Bomb petrified trolls for Bloodgold, cast {{dnKeys}} Intricate Keys, open {{kallMorkhallaNeed}} Mörkhalla, then fetch Kall's summon from the Jotun invasions their ice sends south.

## How do I get far north?

The Ashlands left you at the bottom of the world, and the last biome waits at the very top. Portal home and sail north from there: your home stands by the stones, so the voyage is about as long as Kall is far from the start.

## How do I take the whole workshop north?

Here is the catch that shapes the whole biome: Bloodgold, and the petrified tissue it is smelted from, never pass a wooden portal. So the blast furnace and the Black Forge must stand where the tissue is dug. Everything else travels freely: frost cores, ice, the moulds, the cast keys, the blood. The one gate that does carry ore is the Ashlands' stone portal. Once you can build it on snow-free ground, it ties the north to home.

> **With no portals**, the ship hauls the whole workshop from your last base: the blast furnace's materials, the Black Forge's with its extensions, the frost foundry's iron, and the kit below.

## How do I stand the cold?

The north does not need to hit you to kill you. Every weather here freezes, by day and by night: your health stops coming back, and the frost grinds it down to death. Any frost-resistant cape ends that: the wolf, lox, asksvin and ash capes, the Moose Hide Cape, and the feather cape too, though it is the one to leave home (see what to bring, below). So does a wolf or Fenring chest, any of the three Nord chests, or a [frost resistance mead](/catalog/meads#meadfrostresist). They do not stack, so with a cape on the mead is wasted, and a soaking from the sea will not bring the cold back.

Underground the rules change. The Winding Tunnels are neither cold nor freezing. A Mörkhalla is only cold, so a campfire inside does the job. Kall's prison freezes as hard as the open snow. And the Lox Fur armour, however warm it looks, does nothing against the frost.

## Where do I make camp, and how do I build on snow?

Snow refuses a piece only where it would stand on bare ground. Build on a rock, on the floor of a village house or the Tunnels' hall, or on a piece already standing, and the snow never gets a say. On open snow the hoe is your tool: levelling, raising, a path or a paved road clears the snow under it, while the cultivator only piles on more. The Snow Shovel does the same work later, at the price of a Wooden Battle Idol on top of Fader's embers.

Heavy snow wears down only what stands uncovered. Put a roof over a piece, or a shield generator's dome, and it stays whole at any pitch. The north's own stave pieces, Timberwood at a plain workbench, never take snow damage, the stave gate excepted, and neither do the darkwood roofs: a stave home rides out every storm. Timberwood also stacks taller than any other wood, {{buildHeightStave}} m in a straight stack; every material's height is in [How high can a wall or a lookout stand?](/guides/boss-arenas#how-high-can-a-wall-or-a-lookout-stand).

Pitch the camp by Kall's gate. Everything the hunt needs stands around it, so one walled camp serves every trip, and once he is dead your last home grows out of it. The [Deep North building](/catalog/deep-north-building) pieces list the rest. Nothing grows here, dome or no dome, but the village granaries hold seeds for the farm back home.

## Can I start in the Deep North?

Yes, and it is the wildest way in. A [reverse run](/guides/reverse-run) lands here with the start island's least, on a raft: no boss item is needed to sail north. It has no forge to come back to, so its first camp goes by a Winding Tunnels in a village, as its own page tells, not by Kall's gate. The freeze soon kills a fresh character, so the first thing ashore is a campfire, and it goes on a rock, since deep snow refuses any piece aimed at bare ground. Lit or not, its ring keeps the open world from spawning anything round it ([Boss arenas](/guides/boss-arenas#which-pieces-make-the-ground-a-base)), and Timberwood, the north's own wood, falls to your very first axe.

Fire is the weapon you start with. Most of what walks the snow is weak to it; the Gammeltroll shrugs it off, and the Krigen take it at full strength only.

Start with the seals and keep their pelts: they never fight back, and once you bring an ember from the Ashlands, {{emberChargePelts}} pelts with {{emberChargeEmbers}} ember make {{emberCharges}} Ember Charges. The north feeds you Timberwood, ice, pelts, moose hide and lingonberries, and hands you a tame moose for the price of the berries. Two things it never gives. The embers come from the Ashlands' Eternal Pyre, and the Black Forge wants the Mistlands' black cores and marble. Every key waits on them. The road back, gate by gate, is on the reverse run's page.

## How do I survive the north with one life?

For the player who never dies, on a harder world. Until Kall falls, two raids stalk the north: the Jotnar, once the first Krigen or Hexen dies, and the Eyeless Ones, once the first of them does. Neither can start unless {{raidBasePieces}} base pieces stand around you. So sleep with a campfire and a bed and nothing else. Keep the workbench and the portal more than {{raidClearM}} m from the bed, and remember that a hooded lantern counts as a base piece too.

| Round where you sleep | Base pieces | A raid can come |
|---|---|---|
| A campfire and a bed | {{raidSafePieces}} | no |
| A campfire, a bed and a workbench | {{raidBasePieces}} | yes, until Kall falls |

A base does not stop everything. A Winding Tunnels' wardrobe shaft keeps calling starred Elaking until you break it, and an invasion's core keeps calling starred Jotnar while you stand near it, wall or no wall. The Gammeltroll and the Barka never come starred; the moose, the shaft Elaking and the Mörkhalla's Krigen may. Underground, in a Mörkhalla or the Tunnels, a harder world's creatures keep their normal speed and size, and a Mörkhalla's guards stay dead once killed, so its halls are safer than the snow. ## What do I bring?

A frost-resistant cape, the grappling hook from the [Mistlands](/guides/mistlands) for a Mörkhalla's raised door, the hardest-hitting blunt or slashing weapon you own, embers for Ember Charges, iron for the frost foundry, and meads. The drop to a Mörkhalla's floor deals at most {{fallMaxDamage}} damage from any height, so the jump needs no feather cape, and Kall is the reason to leave it home: it doubles the fire you take, which only a fire resistance mead undoes in his last phase. This is what the [hives at home](/guides/meadows#how-many-hives-for-brewing-all-game) were for. Health, stamina and fire resistance for the fight, frost resistance for the gaps; the [meads](/catalog/meads) give each base.

## Where does the Bloodgold come from?

From the petrified trolls, stone arms and legs half buried in the snow, and nothing but a bomb cracks them. No pickaxe is strong enough, and a living Gammeltroll's punch no longer does it. The bomb is the Ember Charge, made at a workbench from seal pelts and the embers of the [Ashlands](/guides/ashlands)' Eternal Pyre. Part of the rubble is petrified tissue, smelted into Bloodgold in the blast furnace, and a single leg holds about the tissue of every key.

## How do I find the Winding Tunnels, with the moulds?

The Intricate Key's mould lies in the Winding Tunnels, and their entrance hides under an empty timber hall. The Deep North's villages sit within {{villageTunnelM}} m of it or of one another, so spot a village and the Tunnels are near. Inside, the Eyeless One drops a mould half the time it dies. Sneak up on it, since it wakes only when you come close, and know that its first death opens the Eyeless raid. Break every wardrobe shaft you pass: that is what keeps the Elaking coming.

## How do I find the Mörkhalla?

A Mörkhalla is a tower that goes down, not up. Hook up to its raised door, turn the key, and jump. At the bottom, its end room holds Kall's Vegvísir, the Jotnar who guard it, carrying the moulds of the Nord weapons and armour, and the Malicious Ice. You may light a campfire and cook down there, just not at its very centre; a workbench cannot be raised underground.

## Do I have to go back south for the blood?

Yes, and this is the biome's last twist: no Mörkhalla holds the blood. Breaking its Malicious Ice sets a Jotun invasion loose in the middle biomes, the Meadows, Black Forest, Swamp, Mountain or Plains, never the Mistlands. The invasion's core breaks into one Malicious Blood, and the invasion ends with it. Where it lands is drawn the moment the ice breaks, so no seed can tell you in advance, but it keeps to the game's bounds below. You know one by its frozen weather and the Jotnar around it; in the Black Forest an invasion only chills at night.

The core calls starred Krigen and Hexen while you stand near it, and no wall stops them; the invasion's wandering bands and its meteors never fall inside a base's reach or right beside you. Stay for the Hexen: one sometimes drops a finished key.

## How do I find the invasions and the Mörkhalla with no map?

For the player who switched the map off. The snow shows the way: wherever you stand, its flakes drift toward a live invasion, and inside one there are none. With several standing they may lead you to a farther one rather than the nearest, so clear the one they show and follow again. In the north the land does the rest. A village means the Winding Tunnels are close, every Mörkhalla's end room holds Kall's Vegvísir, and Yggdrasil's branch hangs fixed in the sky, a landmark that never moves. Raise a stave tower of Timberwood over the gate, roofed or not, and it stands through every storm as your lighthouse.

## How do I summon and kill Kall?

Pour the blood into the bowls outside his prison and the gate opens; Kall is already waiting inside. Fall, and a new fight is summoned at the bowl within, for the blood again: the gate takes {{maliciousBlood}} Malicious Blood, and every new try {{kallRetryBlood}} more, one from each invasion's core, so each try costs {{kallRetryBlood}} more invasions.

His prison freezes and allows no building, so come with frost cover on and set your portal outside. He takes half from pierce, fire, frost and lightning, never staggers, and in his last phase ignores whatever your tames or summons do: bring blunt or slash and do the work yourself. His blows smash whatever you build. The fight itself is in [How to kill each boss](/catalog/bosses#kall-fimbulbringer).

> **With no death**, come in a Nord set at its top with frost cover and a fire resistance mead, sleep by two base pieces only, hold {{kallRetryBlood}} bloods spare for a second try, and fight the Jotnar in the Mörkhalla rather than the snow.

> **At leisure**, Kall's death ends both raids of the north, and the [Bog Witch](/catalog/traders) sells the seasoning of its last feast: the home by the gate can grow without a guard.

## How do I race through the north?

For the runner on the clock of a [Speedrun](/guides/speedrun). The Ashlands end your run far in the south, so portal to your copper base near the map's middle and sail north from there; the voyage's length comes down to how far Kall stands from the north–south line through the start: {{kallAxisM}} m as a rule, {{kallAxisMLow}}–{{kallAxisMHigh}} m on the middle half of seeds. On {{stripKall}}% of seeds he stands in the [strip](/guides/speedrun#what-is-the-strip).

Then save the trips: break all {{kallMorkhallaNeed}} Malicious Ices in one sweep of the Mörkhalla, portal south, take the cores, and sail north once more.

## Can you skip the Deep North?

Kall is the last boss, and the north is where he lives: every run to Kall comes this far north. Every All Bosses category of the [Speedrun](/guides/speedrun) before Deep North stops short of the snow.

[How good is my seed?](/tools/how-good-is-my-seed) sets your own seed against these numbers, and the [seed map](/tools/map) shows where its places stand. Kall is the end of the run, not of the world. The home you raised in the snow stays standing, and the same seed, played by harder rules, starts the saga over in the [Challenges](/guides/challenges).
