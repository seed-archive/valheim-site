# Speedrun

**How do I speedrun to the end?** Race for iron, forge only what the next boss asks, let monsters chop and mine for you, and sail as little as you can, above all home with a load.

## What counts as a speedrun?

The seed has passed its tests; now the clock starts. Runners race in All Bosses: Classic stops the timer when Yagluth falls, and the later categories push on through the Queen, Fader and Kall Fimbulbringer. A category counts every boss up to its last, so what the sections below say of skipping one is for a run off the board, against your own clock. Each runs on a random seed or a set one, and glitchless lets you outwit a boss but never clip through a wall. Few bosses force the order: an altar wants its offering and nothing else, but the withered bones lie behind the Elder's key, silver wants the iron pickaxe, and the north's key wants Moder's tears and Fader's embers, the gates the [Reverse run](/guides/reverse-run#what-does-each-boss-open-and-how-do-i-get-round-it) lists. Within them the route below is simply the shortest. For Classic, the [Plains](/guides/plains) are the last of the Biomes you need to see. The categories, their rules and today's times are on the [Valheim leaderboards of speedrun.com](https://www.speedrun.com/valheim), and the runner whose runs taught this guide stands in the [credits](/credits).

## What is the strip?

Your run starts in the middle of the world, drops south to the Ashlands, then turns and climbs to the Deep North. The fastest way to do that is to stop wandering: draw one imaginary line through the start, north to south, and keep to it. That line, {{stripM}} m wide, is the strip. Whatever stands in it lies on your road; whatever stands beside it is a detour, and every detour is time off the clock. It is a rule of thumb, not a wall: when the boat has to round an island, you leave the strip and come back.

Wherever another page of the Challenges says "strip" or "on the road", it means this line through the start. It is your line, not the world's. Off the clock you round the island, or go anywhere you like; a reverse run sails north first, keeps to no strip at all and goes where the shore is.

> **With no map**, the coast and the roads you built become your line, and the strip's shares mean little: what counts is what you can see and reach from them.

## What is the speedrun route, boss by boss?

Each biome is one leg of the route. The [Biomes](/guides/biomes) tell them for the player who explores; here is the same land on the clock.

### Meadows

For you the Meadows are a road, not a home: the base waits at a troll cave of the Black Forest, with copper and tin by its mouth.

Eikthyr is killed for speed, never for need. His power cuts what running costs you in stamina, so the scouting of [A speedrunner's start](/guides/a-speedrunners-start) goes quicker, and it pays when his altar lies near your way to the base cave: going by his altar adds {{eikthyrDetourM}} m to that walk as a rule ({{eikthyrDetourMLow}}–{{eikthyrDetourMHigh}} m on the middle half of seeds). With his altar far off the way, a run off the board goes without him; an All Bosses run still owes him the visit and loses only the early power.

The deer for his altar cross your road anyway; carry a spear or a bow for them. Then burn him: ring his altar with campfires. They do not last, and they need not, since the burning they set does the work, and their ground counts as a base ([Boss arenas](/guides/boss-arenas#which-pieces-make-the-ground-a-base)), so nothing of the open world rises round them while he burns. Make the antler pickaxe from his antler and nothing else, and run on.

Take the hives only from the houses you rob on that walk, and turn aside for none: {{hivesFive}} serve a run, which brews only a handful of times, and {{hivesFive}} stand within {{wayReachM}} m of the walk on {{hivesOnWayFive}}% of seeds. Fewer mean a little less mead, not a detour.

Skipping Eikthyr costs more than the walk to him. Without his antler nothing mines before bronze, so a troll's blows must do the digging at every vein, and without his power every step costs full stamina.

### Black Forest

Your copper base is a troll cave on the shore, with copper veins by its mouth and tin at the water's edge, and the cave is the mine: a troll that rises inside or by the entrance breaks the veins for you. Build the hall on open ground in sight of it, and the boat on the water facing it when the time comes to sail. The base cave lies {{baseM}} m from the start as a rule ({{baseMLow}}–{{baseMHigh}} m on the middle half of seeds), and the sea lies within {{caveReach}} m of it on {{baseShore}}% of seeds.

**Making the cave safe.** You sleep by the cave every night, so its trolls are the first thing to settle. Your pieces keep the open world's creatures off ([Boss arenas](/guides/boss-arenas#which-pieces-make-the-ground-a-base)), but nothing you build stops a Greydwarf nest, so set the hall where no camp stands near. The nearest Greydwarf camp lies {{baseCampM}} m from the base cave as a rule ({{baseCampMLow}}–{{baseCampMHigh}} m on the middle half of seeds).

### Swamp

You want the mace, the pickaxe and the boss in one trip, then out. Stop digging once the chests and piles give the mace's iron and the pickaxe's, and count on a second visit for the stations: one more crypt then is quicker than a long dig now. A run to Kall spends {{runIron}} iron in all, the mace and the pickaxe included; the least a run to Kall needs, its gates alone, is {{leastIronDeepNorth}}.

Smelt where the ore lies. A smelter and a forge by the crypt, fed by a surtlings' fire hole's coal and cores, turn the scrap into the mace on the spot, and best of all when the crypt by the fire hole is also the one by his altar, so the iron and the boss are one trip.

Skipping the Swamp has its price: without the wishbone, silver means digging the Mountain blind and hoping Moder's shots dig it out, a poor hope; without iron the forge stays where bronze left it, and the iron pickaxe never comes.

### Mountain

The Mountain is one climb and out. No gate takes silver; mine only the bars the Wisplight wants. Up once on the mead with the iron pickaxe, the walk through the nests and the nearest vein, a smelter right beside her altar so only bars go down, since a cart on her slopes overturns, then Moder, and down again. From her altar and back, the walk through the {{moderEggs}} nearest nests is {{moderEggLoopM}} m as a rule, and {{moderClimbLoopM}} m with the nearest vein added: the silver costs almost nothing extra.

Hang her trophy before the long sails: her power speeds you on foot, lets you carry more and gives the sail a wind.

She cannot be skipped even off the board: without her tears there is no artisan table, so no blast furnace for the Plains' black metal.

### Plains

Yagluth takes {{goblinTotems}} Fuling totems, and you take them the cheapest way: raid the towers first, since an outpost always holds its totem, slip into the villages only for the rest, and pocket the black metal of the Fulings you cut down on the way. Outrun the Deathsquitos or shoot them; the Root Harnesk is a comfort you may not have.

Classic, the shortest category, stops the clock here: it ends at his altar.

Going on, the Plains cannot be skipped: black metal goes into the Galdr Table and the Queen's Sealbreaker, and without Yagluth's Torn Spirit you walk the Mistlands blind in the mist.

### Mistlands

You have no time to grope through the mist. The rules let you print the seed, so look the Infested Mines up on the [seed map](/tools/map) and walk straight to the ones by the Queen: {{queenMinesFew}} or more stand in her own Mistlands on {{queenMines}}% of seeds, and within {{bossNearM}} m of her gate on {{queenMinesNear}}%.

Let a Seeker Soldier do the mining: lead it so its ram and slam land on a giant's ribs, and it breaks them for you, so the remains become a quarry without a swing of your own.

The Mistlands cannot be skipped if you mean to go on: the Black Forge, built with the mines' black cores, forges Fader's bells and casts the Intricate Key, and the Drakkar takes Yggdrasil wood and the ceramic plates that only the Queen's drop makes possible.

### Ashlands

You come for Fader and the north's embers and nothing else: no foothold, no portal, no second trip. Where his altar lies near the water, the Drakkar beaches a short walk from the fight, and the run spends no time crossing burning ground: it stands within {{faderShoreNearM}} m of the coast on {{faderShoreNear}}% of seeds and within {{faderShoreFarM}} m on {{faderShoreFar}}%.

A fortress costs you neither ram nor catapult: a ladder over the wall onto the tower, down through the hole in its roof to the bell fragments, then the tower dug under from four sides until a chest falls out on one of them, while the creatures falling in are killed. Build the Eternal Pyre the moment Fader falls, so its embers are ready for the north's charges, and carry home only the flametal the Black Forge asks.

The Ashlands cannot be skipped on the way to the Deep North: only the embers of Fader's pyre break the petrified trolls, whose tissue is the north's Bloodgold.

### Deep North

The Ashlands end your run far in the south, so portal to your copper base near the map's middle and sail north from there; how far beside the line Kall's gate stands is the run's last detour.

The order is the Winding Tunnels first, for the key's mould, then the Mörkhalla near him. Then save the trips: break all {{kallMorkhallaNeed}} Malicious Ices in one sweep of the Mörkhalla, portal south, take the cores, and sail north once more.

Kall is the last boss, so the north is never skipped by a run that goes to him; every All Bosses category before Deep North stops short of the snow.

How each fight goes, blow by blow, is on [How to kill each boss](/catalog/bosses).

## How do I save time?

**Let creatures do the work.** A troll's blows hit at a bronze tool's tier and above: lure one past birches and oaks and it fells fine wood for you; lead it over copper and it mines. The Elder's blows fell the plain trees round his altar. Later a Seeker Soldier breaks black marble and Yggdrasil wood. The rules allow all of it.

**Skip what changes nothing.** The bronze pickaxe mines nothing the antler one cannot. The Early Axe cuts fine wood without any bronze, from axe heads you find in the Meadows' abandoned houses.

**Hang each power for its leg.** A trophy's power lasts {{powerSeconds}} s and comes back after {{powerCooldownSeconds}} s, so spend each where it pays: Eikthyr's on the scouting, the Elder's while you chop and mine without a troll, Moder's tailwind on the long sail north. Every power is on [Trophies](/catalog/trophies).

**Close each army fast.** A boss's raids begin when the boss before him falls and end when he does, so a quick dash from altar to altar leaves each army little time to come at you. Wolves, skeletons and the rest stay once they have come; the [Raids](/catalog/events) page says what opens and ends each.

**Eat to the clock.** Cook only what the run will eat before it ends: [Food and feasts](/catalog/food) gives each dish's duration.

**Hunt to the recipe.** In the Deep North one seal gives the {{emberChargePelts}} pelts of one batch of {{emberCharges}} Ember Charges, and the charges crack the petrified trolls whose tissue makes Kall's key. Kill one seal for each batch you need, and not one more.

**Plan before the pickaxe.** The [craft tree](/tools/craft-tree) is the whole run on one page: switch on what you will make, and it lists every station level and every raw material it takes, biome by biome, so you know how much you must carry before you set out.

> **With no death**, saving time turns round, and the troll you used as a lumberjack becomes the risk: a cave troll comes with a star on {{starOneSpawnerNormal}}% of spawns, {{starOneSpawnerVeryHard}}% on Very hard, and a star makes its blows {{starDamage1}}% as hard before the Combat setting multiplies them again. The safe run spends that time on a pickaxe, the Root Harnesk and every skill at its top; [Hard, Very hard and Hardcore](/guides/hardcore#how-often-do-creatures-come-starred) gives the rest.

Beat the clock, and the world is not done with you: next it takes away your second life, your map or your portals, one at a time or all at once, in [No death, no map, no portals](/guides/other-rules).
