# A good start

**What is the best start?** One that lets you walk far before you ever need a boat, with a home by the water where the ore boat beaches at your door.

## What is on my starting island?

The Valkyrie drops you by the Sacrificial Stones, the stones every later page counts from, and the first hour here decides how many voyages the rest of the game will cost. Your start island is everything your feet reach from them, with a swim of up to {{swimM}} m on a full belly of Meadows food, the most the stamina carries you before the water takes you under: a river you swim across, the sea you do not. Every biome on it is a voyage you never sail: there, and worse, back with a full hold. Every boss whose altar stands on it dies without a boat. Copper is as good as certain to share the island with you; iron and silver are the metals that will make you build a hull.

## Where do I build my home?

Right here, most likely: most players settle in the Meadows by the stones, where the ore boat can beach at the front door. Before the first beam goes down, find out whether the water by the start opens on the sea or is a pretty lake that keeps every keel you build its prisoner: [Can my ships reach the open sea?](/guides/where-to-build#can-my-ships-reach-the-open-sea) gives the odds.

Every upgrade eats more of the same metal, twice and then four times the first step's, so you will make each haul again and again. A home is judged by how far each metal, and the market, lie from its door.

## Where are my first nights safe?

Light a campfire, drop a bed or a workbench, and you draw a circle the world respects: {{baseRadiusCampfire}} m round the campfire, lit or not, where no wild creature rises, no raider spawns and no placed spawner wakes. Raiders appear {{raidMinM}}–{{raidMaxM}} m out and walk in. A Greydwarf nest ignores the circle and keeps spawning whatever you build, so pitch camp well away from the camps. [Boss arenas](/guides/boss-arenas#which-pieces-make-the-ground-a-base) gives every piece's circle.

Home is kinder about stars: round the world's centre, where you begin, no open-world Boar or Neck carries one within {{starMinDistanceBoar}} m, and no Troll within {{starMinDistanceTroll}} m. A troll cave's own trolls never got the message. They roll their stars at any distance, so the nearest cave is where the danger starts.

## How far can I go without a boat?

To copper, always: the island's [Black Forest](/guides/black-forest) always hides a troll cave with copper veins at its mouth and tin nearby, ore you carry home on your own legs. Silver comes free only when Moder's Mountain shares your island too. Iron is the coin toss. The Sunken Crypts sit only deep inside a [Swamp](/guides/swamp), and a Swamp across a river you can swim belongs to your island as much as the one by the stones; when it is deep enough, the iron walks home with you, and when it is not, the iron gets your boat wet.

| On the start island | How often |
|---|---|
| Copper: a troll cave with copper and tin by it | {{forestIsland}}% of seeds |
| Iron: a Sunken Crypt | {{swampOnFoot}}% of seeds |
| Silver: Moder's altar on her [Mountain](/guides/mountain) | {{islandModer}}% of seeds |

The cores, at least, are no gamble. The island's Burial Chambers must give up {{coresNeed}} surtling cores for the charcoal kiln, the smelter and a pair of portals, and they do on {{islandCoresEnough}}% of seeds: rob them and the smelter is yours.

> **With no portals**, the cores a usual game spends on a portal buy a kiln and a smelter at the next outpost instead: players haul them along with the first iron and copper to wherever they settle next.

## Can I bridge my island to the next?

No wooden portal lets ore or metal through, so every load comes home by sea, and you sail the same voyage again with every upgrade. A strait narrow and shallow enough to bridge is a voyage you never sail again. A pier that stands on the seabed carries its material's full strength: core wood rises about {{buildHeightCoreWood}} m off the bottom, iron about {{buildHeightIron}} m, so the strait's depth picks your wood. Water you swim in {{swimM}} m already counts as your island, so where the table finds no water at all, the place already shares your island and wants no bridge.

To Eikthyr, in the usual order. His altar is the nearest of them all and stands on the start island on {{islandEikthyr}}% of seeds, so his power, cheaper running, costs you next to nothing. Then the Elder, and only then Bonemass and Moder.

## Racing: is this seed worth the run?

This one is for the speedrunner, who does not settle by the stones: he makes his copper base in a troll cave with copper and tin by its mouth, the base cave of the tables below, and his first loop scouts the whole way until he knows where the iron and the silver will come from, pocketing the surtling cores, marking the copper veins, robbing whatever pays in coins. A seed shows whether it deserves your evening on that loop, before it has cost you one. The loop runs start, Eikthyr, the base cave, the Elder, Bonemass, Moder, and its legs are what you actually sail and walk, not the straight lines from the stones; the base cave itself lies {{baseM}} m from the start as a rule, {{baseMLow}}–{{baseMHigh}} m on the middle half of seeds.
