# Reverse run

**How do I play backwards, the Deep North first?** Sail north on a raft, camp by a Winding Tunnels under a roof over a campfire, burn what never fights back, then go south for Moder and Fader.

## What is a reverse run?

This is the road left untried, the game turned round: straight to the [Deep North](/guides/deep-north) with next to nothing, the bosses backwards from the last to the first, and the long way home to the [Meadows](/guides/meadows). Any boss can be summoned once its offering is in hand, so nothing forbids it; what makes it hard is that in the usual order each boss hands you the tool for the next, and here you arrive without it. Biomes can be skipped this way; the game's rules cannot, so the road to Kall still passes the same stations, only in another order. The route below is learned from the players named in the [credits](/credits).

> **Speedrunners** who try it backwards race no board: no category names the order, so a reverse run is timed against itself. The quickest order the game allows starts north for the mould and the scouting, takes the [Mistlands](/guides/mistlands)' forge, Fader in the [Ashlands](/guides/ashlands) and Moder on the [Mountain](/guides/mountain), and only then goes back north for the keys and Kall.

## Which bosses can I not leave behind?

Moder and Fader, and the chain that binds them is short. Everything the Deep North forges is Bloodgold, and Bloodgold melts only in the blast furnace, built at the artisan table, built from Moder's tears. Its ore is petrified tissue, and the petrified trolls break only to Ember Charges, whose embers burn in the Eternal Pyre, built from Fader's ribs. Kall's gate wants {{maliciousBlood}} Malicious Blood; the blood comes from the Jotun invasions a Mörkhalla's ice sets loose, and a Mörkhalla opens only to an Intricate Key cast from Bloodgold. One biome whose boss you may skip is just as fixed: the Mistlands, whose Black Forge casts the key and Fader's {{bells}} bells. The Queen, Yagluth, Bonemass, the Elder and Eikthyr can all wait.

Moder is the long trip, since a reverse run lives at the far end of the world, round Kall's gate:

## What does each boss open, and how do I get round it?

Every boss of the usual order opens a door. Played backwards, each door is got round or paid for, in the order you meet them:

| Gate | What opens it in the usual order | The way round |
|---|---|---|
| Petrified tissue | Ember Charges, of seal pelts and Fader's embers | none: fetch Fader |
| The Mörkhalla's door and Kall's gate | the Intricate Key, of Bloodgold from the blast furnace, built with Moder's tears | none: fetch Moder |
| The key's cast and Fader's bells | the Black Forge, of the Mistlands' black marble, Yggdrasil wood and black cores | none: the Mistlands before both |
| The Mörkhalla's raised entrance | the grappling hook: {{hookYggdrasil}} Yggdrasil wood, {{hookEitr}} eitr, {{hookMandibles}} mandibles and a Hook from the Infested Mines | the Mistlands' mines |
| The Ashlands | the Drakkar, the one boat the boiling sea spares | bridges or swimming between the sea's spires, or the grappling hook |
| The Queen's door | the Sealbreaker, of the Infested Mines' fragments | not needed: skip her |
| Finding silver | Bonemass's wishbone | dig the mountains blind; the Deep North gives no wishbone |
| Bonemass's withered bones | the Elder's key to the Sunken Crypts | the Elder first, even backwards |
| A pickaxe | Eikthyr's hard antler | a troll's blows mine copper and tin for you; or one short walk to Eikthyr first |

Eikthyr is as a rule the nearest boss of the world, and his antler pickaxe breaks the Deep North's ice chunks and frozen statues and the tin you meet on the way back, so the short walk buys more than a pickaxe; setting out without him takes only the raft's wood, leather scraps and resin. The straight fight and the trick for every boss are in [How to kill each boss](/catalog/bosses).

The springs' refined eitr is the hidden gate: it is made only from sap, so the key waits on a stolen Dvergr extractor and an eitr refinery in the [Mistlands](/guides/mistlands#how-do-i-take-a-dvergr-extractor) as well. The forge reaches its level with any of its extensions, so the Smith's Aprons, {{apronBloodgold}} Bloodgold, {{apronTimberwood}} Timberwood and {{apronHides}} moose hides, can stand in for the flametal one at the price of more tissue; their Bloodgold still needs Fader's embers. Every later key costs only the Bloodgold, a new mould and its Liquid Frost.

**Where the iron comes from.** Iron is the quiet gate: the furnace, the stonecutter, the forge's extensions and the foundry all want it, and the Sunken Crypts that give it in the usual order wait behind the Elder. In the north the Winding Tunnels' rubble and wardrobe shafts break into iron scrap now and then, and in the Ashlands the pots hold iron bars. Metal stays where it was made: ore, tissue and bars never pass a wooden portal, so the blast furnace and the foundry stand in the north, and the iron that builds them comes by boat or out of the Tunnels.

> **With no portals**, nothing comes home but by sea, and the Ashlands' stone portal, the one that carries metal, does not exist either: every bar and every station's iron sails, and Moder's tears come back from her Mountain on the same boat as the silver.

## How do I get to the Deep North?

By sea: the ocean cuts it off from every other land, so the boat is the bill, and the start island of [A good start](/guides/a-good-start) is all you build it from. The raft takes no metal and no boss item, and the northern sea does not burn boats as the Ashlands' does, so a run can leave on the first raft the island allows. Its danger is the floating ice along the shore: land with care, and break the sheets for ice.

The Karve's bronze nails wait for copper, tin and something to mine them: Eikthyr's antler, or a troll's blows in the [Black Forest](/guides/black-forest), so some players set out no earlier than that. A raft that leaves before the forge and the [Swamp](/guides/swamp) lands without a frost resistance mead, since its base wants the Swamp's bloodbags and the mead ketill's copper and tin (the cold table below): fire under a roof has to hold off the cold instead.

## What do I fetch from the Black Forest?

The cores, the coal and the fine wood. A reverse run starts in the north but smelts with what grows here: the north's ore wants the smelter, the charcoal kiln for its coal and the blast furnace, and their cores come from the Burial Chambers. The furnace takes {{furnaceFineWood}} fine wood too, from the birches and oaks. Leave a portal here before sailing north, and the cores and the coal follow you through it.

## Where is the iron without the Elder's key?

In what you break on the way. Without his key a Sunken Crypt's door stays shut, and its chests and piles with it. The north asks for iron all the same, {{firstKeyIron}} for its own stations alone, the blast furnace, the frost foundry, the Black Forge's extensions with the Vice's mechanical springs, and the stonecutter ([What to break](/catalog/breakables)).

## How do I live through the first night?

Every weather of the Deep North freezes you, day and night: no health coming back, stamina slower, and a little damage every second until you die. A fire alone or a roof alone softens it to cold; both together lift it, and so does any frost resistance.

| To lift the cold | What it takes | Where it is made |
|---|---|---|
| A campfire under a Timberwood roof | {{campfireStone}} stone, {{campfireWood}} wood; the walls at a workbench of {{workbenchWood}} wood | anywhere off deep snow |
| A frost resistance [mead](/catalog/meads) | {{frostMeadHoney}} honey, {{frostMeadThistle}} thistle, {{frostMeadBloodbags}} bloodbags, {{frostMeadEye}} greydwarf eye; a mead ketill of {{meadCauldronTin}} tin, {{meadCauldronCopper}} copper, {{meadCauldronScraps}} leather scraps | before you sail |
| A feather cape | {{capeFeathers}} feathers, {{capeScaleHides}} scale hides, {{capeEitr}} eitr | after the Mistlands |

Timberwood falls to the first axe, and its stave walls do not wear under snow. Open snow refuses every piece, the campfire too, so set the fire on a rock, on a village house's floor, or on ground the hoe has levelled. The resistances do not add up: with the cape on, the mead is wasted, and the cape doubles the fire you take in the Ashlands. Moder's power from her [trophy](/catalog/trophies) halves the frost you take, but a reverse run hangs it late.

### How do I camp without calling a raid?

A raid on a base is called only when {{raidBasePieces}} base pieces stand round you, counted within {{raidBaseM}} m. A campfire and a bed are two: inside their reach nothing of the open world rises, and no raid comes. Walls, roofs and chests do not count; a workbench, a torch or any station does. Every piece's reach is in [Boss arenas](/guides/boss-arenas#which-pieces-make-the-ground-a-base).

| By your fire | The open world spawns round you | A raid on the camp can be called |
|---|---|---|
| A campfire | not within {{baseRadiusCampfire}} m, lit or not | no |
| A campfire and a bed | not within their reach | no |
| A campfire, a bed and a workbench | not within their reach | yes |

So build the walls and the bed, then take the workbench down. A nest beats every base: a wardrobe shaft in the Tunnels or an invasion's black ice core keeps spawning inside any camp, so camp away from them.

> **With no death**, a reverse run is the opposite of a safe one: the cold stops your health coming back, and the usual order hands you each defence before the biome that needs it. If you try it, keep to two pieces by your fire, a campfire and a bed, and never light a fire for a Gammeltroll: fire does not touch it.

## Where do I set my first camp?

By a Winding Tunnels entrance, in a village. The Tunnels are neither cold nor freezing inside, their rubble and shafts hold the Intricate Key's mould and iron scrap, and the villages gather round them, so a village house gives you a floor the snow cannot refuse. The village granaries hold kale, oat and potato seeds for a farm at home.

Take the Tunnels nearest Kall's gate, where the run ends; from them the Mörkhalla lie close:

## What do I kill first?

The seals on the shore. They never fight back and are weak to fire, so fires under and round a seal kill it with no weapon at all, and its pelts are the half of every Ember Charge that is not Fader's. The moose fights back and falls to fire too; tamed with lingonberries, it is meat without end. The Gammeltroll is the one creature here no fire hurts: walk round it until you have a weapon. Enjoy the quiet while it lasts. The Krigen and the Hexen roam the open world only once a Krigen or a Hexen has died somewhere, and from then on they raid your bases until Kall falls, so the first Jotun you kill is a choice.

One whose far end is tight: Kall's gate with a Winding Tunnels and its Mörkhalla close by, so the north's errand is short, and the far biomes within reach of it.
