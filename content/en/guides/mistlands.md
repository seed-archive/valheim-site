# Mistlands

**How do I explore the Mistlands?** Land where the mist is thin, let the Dvergr kill your first Seekers and dress in what they leave, then comb the mist: giants' remains for marble, mines for cores and the Sealbreaker, a Dvergr extractor for sap, and the Queen's citadel last.

## How far are the Mistlands from home?

Far: the nearest Mistlands lie {{homeMistlandsM}} m from home as a rule, and the Queen's gate {{homeQueenM}} m. Everything you take from here, the black marble and the Yggdrasil wood above all, goes home by boat, so the boat is the Longship and the landing matters.

Sail along the coast before you land, and pick a shore where the mist is thin and the ground low. Set the portal there, outside the deep mist. Bring a spare Wisplight and leave it at the portal: after a death in the mist, the first one lies with your grave, and without a light you will not find either. Bring {{stonecutterIron}} iron too, for a stonecutter on the spot.

## What kills a newcomer in the Mistlands?

The land itself, before anything in it. The mist hides everything past arm's length, and the Wisplight clears only a small circle round you. The ground is cliffs: you climb up and jump down all day, and a fall kills.

The Seeker is the creature of the place, and your whole arsenal is wrong for it: it resists the blade, the club and the arrow alike. Fire and frost are not resisted, so Frostner and frost or fire arrows are what you fight it with at first. Seekers fly, so a height is no refuge; meet them one at a time, never at a cliff's edge. A Seeker Soldier has many times a Seeker's health and a soft abdomen behind its armour. The Gjall floats above the mist and spits fire: shoot its belly from cover. Ticks jump on your back and drink; a parry or a roll throws them off, and guides are split on which.

The Dvergr are the exception. They leave a stranger alone, and they kill Seekers on sight, which is your way in. Lead the Seekers that hunt you to a Dvergr post and stand back: the Dvergr do the killing, and the carapace left on the ground is yours. It makes your first pieces of the land's own armour.

## How many mines does the Sealbreaker take?

As many as it takes to find {{sealbreakerFragments}} fragment crystals: each breaks into one fragment, so you count crystals, not mines. Her own Mistlands hold {{queenFragments}} crystals in {{queenMinesIn}} mines as a rule, and enough of them on {{queenFragmentsEnough}}% of seeds. On the others the last crystals wait in a second Mistlands, and it is worth learning that before you settle by her gate.

## Where do I get black marble and black cores for the stations?

The marble from the giants. Their remains lie across the land, and the black metal pickaxe breaks the ribs and skulls into black marble, the strongest stone you have built with yet; the skulls hold soft tissue as well. The stonecutter turns the Dvergr's own broken pieces into more.

The cores from the mines, and three stations want them:

With too few, build the forge first; a station can be taken down and its cores used again for the next. Set the refinery away from your walls: it throws sparks that break what stands near.

## How do I take a Dvergr extractor?

The sap extractor is built with one, and the Dvergr keep it in a long crate in their towers, lighthouses, excavations and harbours. There are two ways to it, and they cost different things.

The quiet way leaves the Dvergr your friends. The crate must break without your hand in it: anything you hit, throw or shoot at it is yours, and the camp turns on you. A cart rammed into the crate is not yours. A creature led into it is not yours either, and the Dvergr turn on the creature. Later, refined eitr dropped beside a crate cracks it with its own sparks.

The loud way removes the keepers. Lead a Gjall onto the Dvergr post and let it burn them out; it may burn you too, and the post is no shelter afterwards. You gain the crate at once and lose the neighbours who killed your Seekers.

On a Yggdrasil root: a workbench, the sap extractor and a portal, and you come back for the sap. Sap and soft tissue become refined eitr in the refinery, and eitr is what the land's best gear is made with.

Both are for the cliffs. The cape takes the fall away, so you jump down instead of climbing; the hook pulls you up.

With the stations up, the land offers two roads. Carapace and steel: the armour takes {{killsSeeker}} Seekers' carapace, and the weapons are the Mistwalker, Krom, the Demolisher and the arbalest. Or eitr-weave and the staffs: little armour, and magic that the Seekers do not resist, fed by eitr food in place of some of your health. The [armour](/catalog/armour) page has both.

## What does the Queen's fight take?

The Sealbreaker opens her citadel, and she is fought inside it. She resists pierce, strikes with claws and poison, and calls her brood down on you. Krom and the Demolisher do the most to her, then the arbalest with carapace bolts. Drink the poison resistance mead before the door, and carry healing and stamina meads from the hives at home. The citadel's entrance is ground she does not reach: step back there to drink. Every way to kill her is on [How to kill each boss](/catalog/bosses#the-queen).

Her death gives you the majestic carapace, which builds the artisan press, and the press makes ceramic plates: {{drakkarPlates}} of them, with {{drakkarYggdrasil}} Yggdrasil wood and {{drakkarNails}} iron nails, are the Drakkar. Her power brings your eitr back {{queenPowerEitr}}% faster and takes {{queenPowerPoison}}% off poison, for {{powerMinutes}} minutes. The Seekers and the Gjall, who raided your bases from the day Yagluth died, stop.

You leave the mist in carapace or in eitr-weave, with the feather cape, the grappling hook, three new stations and a ship of ceramic and Yggdrasil wood. It was built for one sea, the one that burns: the [Ashlands](/guides/ashlands). On the clock the Mistlands are the mines nearest her gate and nothing else, and the [Speedrun](/guides/speedrun#mistlands) tells it.
