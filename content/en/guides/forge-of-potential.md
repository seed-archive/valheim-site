# Forge of Potential

**Can I upgrade my gear past its last level?** Yes, at the Forge of Potential: a try raises an item one level or shatters it, and your character's name decides which, so the right name never shatters it.

## Where is the Forge of Potential?

On a Mountain, one forge to a world, but not in one fixed spot: the world holds several places where it may stand, and the first of them you come near becomes the forge, while the others are gone for good. The nearest of those places stands {{homeForgeM}} m from your home as a rule, {{homeForgeMLow}}–{{homeForgeMHigh}} m on the middle half of seeds, so it is a trip you plan, not one you pass on the way. The [seed map](/tools/map) marks every place it may stand; head for the nearest, and that is where you will find it.

## What can I upgrade there?

Every weapon, piece of armour, shield, cape and tool that has more than one level: the forge takes any item whose recipe asks for an idol, and all of them do but one. The [Helmet of the Protector](/bugs#the-helmet-of-the-protector-never-reaches-the-forge-of-potential) asks for none, so it never reaches the forge. What has a single level stays as it is: trinkets, bombs, the Demister, tankards, the grappling hook.

Raise an item to its last level at its own workbench or forge first, with the usual materials. The Forge of Potential takes an item at any level, but every try there is a roll, even one the workbench would have made for certain.

## What does a try take?

One idol of the item's tier and nothing else: a Battle idol for a weapon or a tool, a Protection idol for armour, a shield or a cape. The tier follows the biome the item belongs to, from the Wooden idols of the Meadows to the Bloodgold idols of the Deep North. Idols lie in chests in the places of their biome: the Meadows' abandoned houses, the Black Forest's ruins and the Swamp's huts, the Mountain's caves and cabins, the Plains' huts, the towers of the Mistlands, the Ashlands' fortresses and ruins, and the Deep North's shipwrecks and the halls of Mörkhalla; [Caves and dungeons](/catalog/dungeons) lists what each place holds.

## What are the odds?

A try raises the item a level {{success}}% of the time and shatters it {{shatter}}%; there is nothing between.

## What do I get back when it breaks?

Far less than you put in. Of each material, the game adds what the item's first level took to what its current level asks for, and gives back {{returned}}% of that sum, rounded up. Every level in between is lost, and so are the item and its idol.

What a level "asks for" is not doubled each time. A recipe names, for each material, an amount to craft and an amount per level; an upgrade costs the amount per level times a step, and the step is the level less one up to level 3, then 4 at level 4, and half more for each level after. So the cost doubles from level 3 to level 4 and then grows by only half a step a level. The Iron Sword's table shows each level, its step and its cost, then what comes back.

So a break costs more the higher the item stands: every level between the first and the last is gone whole. That is the loss the right name spares you.

## Is it luck?

No. The game draws the outcome of your first try of an item at a level from three things: your character's name, the item and the level you aim for. Nothing else enters it: not your skill, not the world, not the hour. After each try the game moves that item and level on to its next draw and saves it with the character, so reloading the world changes nothing, and another character, with another name, gets other answers for the same item.

So the first try is known before you press the button, if you know the name. We do: the [Nidavellir](/tools/nidavellir) tool takes an item and a step, up to level {{top}}, and gives the names whose first try raises it.

## How do I raise an item without breaking it?

One step at a time, each by a fresh character of its own. Your main character does the gathering; the fresh ones only carry the item to the forge.

1. Raise the item to its last level the usual way, with your main character.
2. Fill a chest at your base for the fresh one: the item, one idol of its tier, a set of the recipe's ingredients, and clothes warm enough for the Mountain.
3. In the [Nidavellir](/tools/nidavellir) tool, pick the item and the next step, and make a new character with one of the names it gives, written exactly, capitals too.
4. Enter your world with it, empty the chest and walk past the station that makes the item, the one at the level its recipe asks for.
5. Go to the forge and press the button once.
6. Bring everything back to the chest. For the next level, ask the tool again and make the next character.

A name holds for the first try of each item at each level, so one character serves any number of items and steps. A second try of the same item at the same level is a new draw, and the tool no longer knows it.

So a character never learns the way up. Each level keeps its own count of tries: a second copy of the same item meets every level on its second try, and may break at a level the first copy passed. A name that took one copy to level 10 can shatter the next at the first step. Every copy goes up with fresh characters, one a step. Delete the character and make it again with the same name, and its tries start over: the first try is the same as before.

## Why does the new character need the ingredients?

The forge lists only the recipes a character knows, and a fresh character knows none. The game teaches a recipe on its own once two things are true: every ingredient of it, the idol included, has been in the character's hands, and the character has stood by the station that makes it, at the level the recipe needs. Picking the ingredients out of the chest and walking past your own forge or workbench does both; nothing has to be crafted, and no skill counts at the Forge of Potential.

## How do I hand an item to another character?

Through the world, not the character: what a character carries goes wherever that character goes, so the item has to be left in the world for the next one to pick up. Put the item and what goes with it in a chest, quit to the main menu, choose the other character, enter the same world and take them out. An item keeps its level in the chest, and any of your characters can enter any of your worlds.

## Which item should I bring?

The gear of your last biome is the gear worth raising: anything older you will put away before its extra levels pay. With a name from the tool the try itself costs nothing but the idol, so the trip is planned around the idols, one for every step.

What a level adds to each item stands in its row, under Each upgrade, on [Weapons](/catalog/weapons), [Staffs](/catalog/staffs) and [Armour](/catalog/armour).
