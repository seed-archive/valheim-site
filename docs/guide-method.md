# Writing a game's guides: the method (for every game of the archive)

Written by the Valheim session on the owner's word, 2026-11-21, for the coordinator to pass to the other games. It
is what Valheim does before rewriting its seventeen guides; Valheim's own findings and plan are in
`guide-writing.md` beside this file. The owner has not yet read a page made this way: the method is a plan, not a
proven recipe.

## Why

Valheim's guides went through a forum of player personas, a judge, a style pass and an SEO pass, and the owner
still could not read past half of the first page. The causes were not in the sentences:

- **The pages answered another reader's questions.** They were planned for the speedrun, then re-voiced for the
  usual player without being derived again, so they told him what a run measures and left out what he lives by.
- **The story was bent to fit the data.** Every measure the generator made got a sentence and a table, and facts
  were invented to carry them (a home placed where the distances happened to be counted from).
- **A page was a pile of separate answers**, each written as if nothing came before, so events happened twice and
  sections contradicted each other.
- **No one owned the whole page.** Each pass looked at its own part; no one read the page in order as its reader.

## The method

1. **Name the reader.** One baseline reader a guide section, said in a sentence (Valheim: a player going through
   the game at his own pace). Every other way of playing has pages of its own and stays off the baseline's.
2. **Write the game down before telling it.** In the generator's notes, for each chapter, how the baseline reader
   lives that part of the game, each fact with its source:
   - the game's own guidance (tutorial and hint texts): the path its makers meant;
   - the game's data: what the part gives, what is made from it, what is dangerous;
   - players' written guides, read as text by a `web-reader`: what players in fact do.

   Nothing is claimed because it suits a measure.
3. **A ledger a chapter**: what the reader has when he arrives, what he does in order, what he has when he leaves.
   A chapter starts from the previous exit; each event happens once. The owner reads all the ledgers together
   before any chapter is written.
4. **A fact earns its place by a decision of this reader.** A number, table or chart that changes nothing he does
   goes to the page of the player it serves, or stays in the tool that rates a seed. One value is a sentence; a
   table has three rows or more of one kind.
5. **One author writes the whole page** from its ledger, in the order the reader lives it. Agents bring facts,
   never sentences.
6. **The check before the owner sees a page**: a fixed list of questions, each to be answered no, read top to
   bottom after the previous chapter (anything twice? anything the ledger does not give? another reader's matter?
   a table under three rows? a claim without a source? a number that decides nothing? a joke where a fact should
   be?). One independent read-only reader answers the same list. Every remark of the owner becomes a question of
   the list.
7. **Two gates before the bulk**: the ledgers, then the first chapter. The rest is written after both pass.

## What a game needs to start

- Its guide pages, read whole, with counts that show the faults (tables by rows, repeated events, statistics per
  page).
- The notes on how its game is played, and an honest look at whose game they describe.
- Its catalogue data by chapter, so a chapter links a list instead of copying it.
